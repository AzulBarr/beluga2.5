\title{Why a MAP Trajectory Need Not Determine Belief-Weighted Loop Verification}
\author{}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
Let $H_1,\ldots,H_M$ be retained trajectory hypotheses, with weights $w_h\geq0$
and $\sum_h w_h=1$. For a fixed loop candidate $c$, let $e_h(c)\in[0,1]$ denote
its trajectory-prior compatibility under $H_h$. These are bounded scores, not
assumed probabilities. Let $m$ select a maximum-weight hypothesis with a fixed
tie rule. Define $S(c)=\sum_h w_he_h(c)$ and accept when $S(c)\geq\tau$, where
$\tau\in(0,1)$. A MAP summary retains $H_m,w_m,e_m(c)$ but not the other
hypotheses' candidate compatibilities.
\begin{proposition}[Information lost by MAP compression]
For every such belief,
\[
 w_me_m(c)\leq S(c)\leq w_me_m(c)+1-w_m,
 \qquad |S(c)-e_m(c)|\leq1-w_m.
\]
Both interval endpoints are attainable. If
$w_me_m(c)<\tau\leq w_me_m(c)+1-w_m$, two assignments of the non-MAP
compatibilities yield the same weights and MAP compatibility but opposite
threshold decisions. Thus the MAP summary cannot determine the full-belief
decision for all admissible compatibility assignments.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because $0\leq e_h(c)\leq1$, the non-MAP contribution
$\sum_{h\ne m}w_he_h(c)$ lies in $[0,1-w_m]$. Assigning every non-MAP
compatibility zero or one attains the lower or upper endpoint. Under the stated
condition these assignments lie on different sides of the acceptance threshold,
while leaving the MAP summary unchanged. Also,
$S(c)-e_m(c)=\sum_{h\ne m}w_h(e_h(c)-e_m(c))$, whose absolute value is at most
$\sum_{h\ne m}w_h=1-w_m$.
\end{proof}
For example, take $(w_1,w_2)=(0.6,0.4)$, $e_1(c)=0$, and $\tau=0.25$.
Then $e_2(c)=1$ yields $S(c)=0.4$ and acceptance, whereas $e_2(c)=0$ yields
$S(c)=0$ and rejection. The MAP compatibility remains zero.

This elementary result motivates retaining non-MAP information; it does not
establish novelty, calibrated posteriors, correct loop labels or improved SLAM
accuracy. Those claims require separate modeling and experimental evidence.
